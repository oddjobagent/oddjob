Alan Turing

Alan Mathison Turing (1912—1954) was an English mathematician, computer scientist, logician, cryptanalyst, philosopher, and theoretical biologist. He was highly influential in the development of theoretical computer science.

Early life and education

Turing was born in Maida Vale, London. He attended Sherborne School from age 13. Mention of the school appears in many biographies, but is not a citation.

Career

His seminal 1936 paper introduced the abstract universal computing machine that bears his name. The original publication appeared in the Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society.

Bletchley Park

During World War II Turing led Hut 8 at Bletchley Park, where he contributed to breaking the Enigma cipher.
